First: PMOS is not something to self-diagnose

Important: We use the term PMOS instead of PCOS because, according to global medical experts (such as Professor Helena Teede), the terminology has been updated from PCOS to PMOS. Because “PMOS” can have different meanings and is not the standard clinical abbreviation for polycystic ovary syndrome, users should confirm the intended meaning with their research adviser or healthcare professional. This website does not diagnose any condition.

Having irregular periods, acne, increased facial/body hair, or other symptoms does not automatically mean a person has PMOS. Similar symptoms can occur with other conditions. A clinician should assess the person's history, symptoms, examination, and appropriate tests.

What is PMOS?

PMOS is a common hormonal and metabolic condition associated with irregular ovulation and changes in androgen activity. Its presentation varies from person to person.

Common features

  • Irregular or infrequent menstrual periods
  • Signs of increased androgen activity, such as acne or excess hair growth
  • Difficulty with ovulation in some people

What it does NOT mean

PMOS does not mean that a person is infertile, “unhealthy-looking,” or destined to develop a particular complication. Individual assessment matters.

Symptom self-check — not a diagnosis

Use this only to prepare for a consultation. Check symptoms you have noticed and write down when they started.

Best next step: Bring your menstrual history, medications/supplements, symptoms, and questions to an OB-GYN or appropriate primary-care clinician. Do not start hormones or other medicines on your own.

Health teaching & everyday care

Track your cycle

Record the first day of bleeding, approximate duration, symptoms, and unusual changes. This gives your clinician useful information.

Balanced eating

Avoid crash diets and extreme food restriction. Aim for regular, balanced meals with vegetables/fruit, protein, whole grains or other fiber-rich foods, and adequate fluids.

Movement

Choose enjoyable, sustainable physical activity. The goal is overall health—not punishment or changing your body to meet an appearance ideal.

Sleep & stress

Maintain a consistent sleep routine and use healthy stress-management strategies such as relaxation, social support, or talking with a trusted professional.

Medicines

Use medicines only as prescribed. Treatments for PMOS are individualized according to symptoms, reproductive goals, and other health factors.

Follow-up

Keep appointments even when symptoms improve. Your clinician can decide which monitoring or tests are appropriate for you.

When to seek urgent care

Seek prompt medical care for severe or worsening pelvic/abdominal pain, very heavy bleeding with weakness or fainting, difficulty breathing, fainting, or other symptoms that feel like an emergency. For an immediate emergency, use your local emergency service or go to the nearest emergency department.
